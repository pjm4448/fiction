# Mother of Toads Reimagined
(After Clark Ashton Smith)

**Synopsis**

Pierre, a young apothecary's apprentice in medieval Averoigne, harbors a shameful secret: while other men desire warm, dry-skinned women, he finds himself perversely drawn to cold, moist textures and amphibian qualities. When his master sends him to the marsh-dwelling witch Mere Antoinette, called the Mother of Toads, Pierre discovers in her batrachian physiology the fulfillment of his darkest fantasies. Against all reason and his master's cryptic warnings about "planting in mud," Pierre willingly enters into carnal congress with the creature, spending a night of grotesque pleasure in her humid, pebbled flesh. But dawn reveals the true horror of what he has coupled with, and when Pierre attempts to flee, an army of toads drives him deeper into the marsh toward a fate that transforms him from willing participant to unwilling vessel in an ancient, chthonic cycle of generation. Clark Ashton Smith's baroque prose style elevates this tale of biological horror and deviant desire into a meditation on the terrible prices exacted when human appetites align with inhuman purposes.

## III. The Reckoning

Dawn came grey and sepulchral. Pierre woke to find Antoinette's arm still draped across him, massive and heavy as a fallen log. The revulsion that now gripped him was merely his human mind's feeble protest against what his body had accepted and celebrated with atavistic fervor.

The path should have been familiar, he had walked it a dozen times, but now every landmark seemed obscured or altered, as if the very geography had shifted during the night. Pierre stumbled forward, desperate to reach the village, to wash himself in ordinary water, to forget this hideous transaction.
